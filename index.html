AI in your course

  • Thursday 29 October 2026
  • 2.30 to 4.00 pm

A practical, hands-on session on using GenAI to build learning resources for your students. To get ideas flowing, we’ll look at a few examples: a simulation game, an audit trail tool and a term planner. Then it’s your turn, with time to experiment and start creating something for your own course.

No prior experience with AI tools is needed. Bring your laptop, and a teaching idea or challenge you’d like to work on. If you don’t have a laptop, you can work with a colleague.

Part 2

Your turn: use GenAI to make learning resources you can keep

Set up a Claude Project with your own course materials, then use it to make an interactive activity or an animated explainer video for your students. Everything you make is a file you download and keep.

Start with the digital worksheet

It takes you through this part of the session, step by step.

Open the worksheet

You’ll need an LSE Claude account

To do the activities, you need an LSE Claude account. If you don’t have one, request one with this form. Accounts aren’t activated straight away, so for today, work with a colleague who already has one.

If you have an account but see “Access denied” when you open shared resources, you’re probably not signed in with SSO. Follow the steps on Troubleshooting in the worksheet.

  • Digital worksheet Steps, prompts to copy and examples, in 14 screens.
  • Printed companion (PDF, 4 pages) The steps on paper, so you can follow the instructions while using Claude on a small screen, without switching tabs too often. It tells you which worksheet screen has each prompt to copy.
  • Accessible PDF version of the worksheet The full worksheet as a tagged PDF, for screen readers and other assistive technology, with all the prompts written out.

How the Part 2 resources were made

Steven Williams made the resources for Part 2 with Claude, in a single chat, as one pack of linked resources: the digital worksheet, the slides, an accessible PDF version of the worksheet and a printed companion. Making them together meant each one could point to the others. The slides show which worksheet screen to open, the printed companion tells you where to find each prompt, and the accessible PDF follows the worksheet screen by screen.

The slides were made with Claude’s new Slides feature, which lets you edit the content of a deck directly in Claude while also asking Claude to make changes. Small fixes can be made by hand, and bigger changes described to Claude.

Each resource was then exported from Claude as an HTML or PDF file and hosted here on GitHub Pages, so none of them depends on a Claude account to open.
